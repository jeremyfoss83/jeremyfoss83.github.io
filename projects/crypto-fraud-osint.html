OSINT · Case Study 02

Public Warning Signs of Cryptocurrency Investment Fraud

A source-driven intelligence report distinguishing fraud indicators, regulatory guidance, allegations, enforcement actions, and final legal findings.

CourseOpen Source Intelligence
TermSummer 2026
CollectionLawful · Passive · Public
OutcomeCourse grade: A

The question

Cryptocurrency investment scams often reuse recognizable tactics, but public discussions can blur allegations, educational warnings, and adjudicated facts. The project focused on identifying useful warning signs without overstating what the source record established.

Collection and validation

I collected public information from the FBI, IC3, SEC, CFTC, DOJ, and related government sources. Each source was evaluated for ownership, date, relevance, and reliability before inclusion.

  • Established a collection plan and defined passive-research boundaries.
  • Compared claims across multiple official sources.
  • Tracked dates and publication context.
  • Separated accusations and enforcement activity from final findings.
  • Recorded gaps and limitations rather than filling them with assumptions.

Reporting discipline

The final deliverable used an intelligence-style structure to synthesize common scam methods and publicly documented warning indicators. The goal was usable analysis with a transparent evidence trail—not a dramatic narrative.

Ethical boundary: No false personas, target contact, system probing, account access, or intrusive collection methods were used.

What I would improve next

I would add a formal source register, define confidence language before drafting, and create a repeatable taxonomy for mapping each warning sign to supporting government publications.

Skills demonstrated

  • Cross-source comparison
  • Legal-status distinctions
  • Risk communication
