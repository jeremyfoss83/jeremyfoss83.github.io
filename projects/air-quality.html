Air Quality Analysis

A classroom analysis of ozone patterns that starts with a basic question: what changes when the data is incomplete?

ContextUSF coursework
TermSeptember 2026
ToolsR · R Markdown
DeliverableHTML analysis

The question

How are ozone measurements distributed, and how do they vary across months? I used the airquality dataset to explore those questions while accounting for missing observations.

My approach

I checked the data before plotting it, identified missing values, created a date variable, and used charts to examine patterns.

  • Identified 37 missing Ozone values and 7 missing Solar.R values.
  • Applied mean imputation and explained the limitations of that choice.
  • Built a histogram of ozone and a boxplot comparing months.
  • Combined code, charts, and interpretation in a knitted HTML report.

The decision that matters

Replacing missing values with the mean makes a dataset easier to analyze, but it can reduce apparent variation and affect relationships between variables. I documented that tradeoff rather than treating the filled values as actual measurements.

What I delivered

Completed and submitted an HTML analysis with visualizations, written interpretation, and an AI-use declaration. This is a coursework project, not a claim about present-day air quality.

What I would improve next

Compare the results with a complete-case analysis and another missing-data method, then check how much the conclusions change.

Skills demonstrated

  • Data cleaning
  • Missing-value handling
  • Data visualization
  • R Markdown reporting
  • Interpreting limitations
